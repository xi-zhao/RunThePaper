# Fixed-version derivation and applicability

Source: arXiv:1612.02058v3, local `source/fixed.pdf` and matching TeX. No later revision or author's numerical implementation is used. All statements below concern this version only. Symbols follow the paper unless an explicit repair is stated.

## Richardson extrapolation and bound

For the propagator U satisfying dU/dt = -i K(t) U, use rho_I = U† rho U. Then d rho_I/dt = lambda L_I(t) rho_I, with L_I(X)=U† L(U X U†) U. Iterating its Volterra equation n+1 times leaves an ordered simplex integral with volume T^(n+1)/(n+1)!. If the trace norm of every product of n+1 interaction-picture generators acting on the intermediate physical density matrix is at most l_(n+1), Holder's inequality gives |R_(n+1)| <= ||A|| l_(n+1)(lambda T)^(n+1)/(n+1)!. Bounded finite-dimensional Lindblad generators and the finite qubit bath used here satisfy a bound of this form. No claim is made that arbitrary unbounded baths satisfy it without domain and moment assumptions.

The time change t=c s gives d rho'(c s)/ds = -i[K(s),rho']+c lambda L(rho'), exactly. This requires a time-independent generator unchanged by the control rescaling. It includes the full system+bath Hamiltonian generator if the entire uncontrolled V is multiplied by lambda. It does not justify separately holding an unscaled bath Hamiltonian fixed while scaling only the coupling.

Lagrange interpolation at lambda=0 gives weights gamma_j = product_(m!=j) c_m/(c_m-c_j). Hence sum gamma_j c_j^k = delta_(k,0) for k=0,...,n. The coefficients solve Eq. (4), cancel the first n terms and give Eq. (6) after summing absolute remainders and shot errors. The use of Gamma_n=sum |gamma_j| c_j^(n+1) for both terms is conservative because c_j>=1.

**Literal counterexample:** the product immediately after Eq. (6) instead prints denominator (c_j-c_m). It differs by (-1)^n. For c=(1,2), the printed coefficients are (-2,1): their sum is -1. The constant function E(lambda)=1 yields -1 instead of 1, an absolute error 2 at every noise rate. The Eq. (4) solution (2,-1) preserves the constant. At n=2 the two formulas coincide, an unaffected control. This is an exact algebraic discrepancy, not rounding, a different random seed or uncertain author data. It does not refute Eq. (4) or the Richardson method.

The main-text depolarizing generator also prints -lambda (I_i tr_i(rho)/2-rho). Applied to |0><0| for time t>0 it gives eigenvalues (1+exp(lambda t))/2 and (1-exp(lambda t))/2, with the latter negative. Supplement I supplies the physical positive sign; generation explicitly uses that sign. An initially mixed state I/2 is an unaffected control. The supplement's interaction-picture definition also reverses U and U† relative to its explicitly negative-i propagator; the derivation above corrects this convention rather than importing it into code.

## Figure 1 and the bath's leading order

The original specifies N=4, six independently rotated drift steps of duration 2, random graph Gaussian X_i Z_j couplings, a random Pauli observable, epsilon in [0.001,0.01], and lambda=-log(1-epsilon)/2. It does not provide the actual graph, coupling variance/seed, control rotations, initial system state, Pauli observable, rescaling nodes, beta, or absolute mapping of lambda to both damping rates. `config.json` declares our reproducible choices. These curves test the stated model family and cancellation mechanism; they are not the undisclosed original realization. No exact pointwise matching is claimed.

For the literal finite-bath model H=K(t) tensor I + lambda V, V=sum_i (X_i X_bi+Z_bi)/2 and a bath state diagonal in Z, tr_b(X_bi rho_B)=0. First-order perturbation of any system-only observable is therefore zero: the system coupling has zero bath mean and the bath-only commutator vanishes under partial trace. Thus E(lambda)=E(0)+a2 lambda^2+... . This is independent of graph, system state and beta, as long as the bath is initially uncorrelated and diagonal. Thermal normalization is explicitly chosen as exp(-beta Z/2)/(2 cosh(beta/2)); the paper's exp(-beta sigma_z) notation with cosh(beta/2) is ambiguous if sigma_z denotes a Pauli matrix. Neither diagonal convention changes the vanishing first derivative.

An ordinary n=1 Richardson step cancels a nonexistent first-order term and multiplies a2 by -c1; it may increase this bath error. n=2 then removes a2; n=3 removes the cubic term. This prediction is checked using a wavefunction coefficient hierarchy, a distinct perturbative calculation, and direct finite-noise propagation. A curve with a linear leading term cannot be explained by changing the unknown random seed within this literal model. However a slope over a finite interval is not by itself a proof of an asymptotic derivative, and the author's actual noise-parameter implementation is unavailable. A later finite-range necessary inequality, derived in `checks/bath_consistency_proof.md` and evaluated in `checks/bath_consistency_bound.json`, excludes the conjunction of the literal model and the displayed absolute-error curve even after conservative reading uncertainty. The author's actual convention remains unidentified; no particular implementation bug is inferred. A square-root coupling convention would change the model and is not substituted for the requested source.

## Quasi-probability cancellation

Let U=sum eta_alpha O_alpha, gamma=sum |eta_alpha|, p=|eta|/gamma and s=sign(eta). Taking the trace fixes sum eta=1, so gamma>=1. Multiplying these identities in circuit order yields the product QPR with multiplicative gamma and sign. A bounded measurement a in [-1,1] has signed estimator Y=gamma s a, E[Y]=Eideal and Var(Y)<=gamma^2. Hoeffding yields P(|mean Y-Eideal|>=delta)<=2 exp(-M delta^2/(2 gamma^2)); a specified failure probability requires the corresponding logarithmic factor. Eq. (9) gives the scaling at fixed success probability, not a failure-probability-free guarantee.

For k-qubit depolarization with d=2^k, D=(1-epsilon)I+epsilon R, where R(rho)=I tr(rho)/d, the inverse is (I-epsilon R)/(1-epsilon). Pauli twirling gives R=d^-2 sum_P P. Thus eta_I=[1-epsilon/d^2]/(1-epsilon), and eta_(P!=I)=-epsilon/[d^2(1-epsilon)]. The l1 norm is gamma_k=[1+(1-2/d^2)epsilon]/(1-epsilon). Normalized negative weights reproduce the paper's p1 and p2. The first-order circuit overhead is 1+epsilon(3 L1/2+15 L2/8); using this first-order truncation at epsilon L~1 is inappropriate. The exact expression is used in all simulations.

For Figure 2, L1=60 and L2=30; gamma=gamma1^60 gamma2^30. We initialize |+>^6, as specified in the dependent numerical supplement, include noise after I gates, and choose the **largest** 32 ideal probabilities, as required by the main caption and supplement prose. The supplement's `arg min` is inconsistent with those instructions and with its Eideal>=1/2 statement; it should be `arg max`.

The generation computes the exact joint distribution of the estimator sign and binary projector outcome, then samples 4000 shots from it. This avoids enumerating exponentially many random circuits without changing the one-shot estimator distribution. The unsigned QPR Pauli mixture followed by physical depolarization is itself depolarization with shrinkage (1-epsilon)(1-4^k p_k). Let q be the final projector probability under that unsigned mixture. Since E[s a]=Eideal/gamma, probabilities of Y/gamma = +1,-1,0 are (q+Eideal/gamma)/2, (q-Eideal/gamma)/2, and 1-q. Mean and variance are exactly Eideal and gamma^2 q-Eideal^2. A separate exhaustive small-circuit enumeration and direct noisy-gate calculation check this reduction.

The actual original numerical supplement uses K sampled QPR circuits and allocations M_j, with sum M_j=4000, and recommends M_j proportional to sqrt(p_j(1-p_j)). It does not report K, how pilot shots enter the budget, or rounding rules. Our K=M=4000 choice is exactly the main-text Eq. (10) and a valid special case of the displayed grouped estimator, but does not recreate its undisclosed optimization. Conditional on sampled circuits, shot variance is gamma^2/K^2 sum p_j(1-p_j)/M_j, minimized by the stated allocation via Cauchy-Schwarz. Across circuits there remains gamma^2 Var(s p)/K. If shot counts are chosen using the same outcome samples later averaged, unbiasedness needs additional care; the paper's statement is secure for predetermined allocations or independent pilots and production samples. We do not assume an unstated data-dependent scheme is unbiased.

## Amplitude damping QPR and preserved depth

For Kraus amplitude damping, its affine Bloch map is (x,y,z)->(s x,s y,(1-epsilon)z+epsilon), s=sqrt(1-epsilon). Every noisy unitary A V has the same nonzero translation. Any trace-preserving linear combination has coefficients summing to 1, so that translation cannot disappear. This establishes the single-operation no-go without state preparations under the stated basis restriction.

The inverse decomposition in supplement VI uses eta1=1/s, eta2=eta3=(1-s)/(2(1-epsilon)), eta4=-epsilon/(1-epsilon) for I,S,S^-1,P0. Direct action on the four Pauli basis matrices verifies it. The l1 norm is (1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon). Tensoring gives the CNOT correction cost gamma^2; resets commute with amplitude damping and can be merged into the following single-qubit gates. H P0=Pplus, and I/S/T P0=P0. The noisy preparation decomposition for Pplus with the paper's three coefficients has l1 norm <=gamma. Thus the full depth is preserved if each CNOT is followed by the assumed single-qubit layer, with overhead <=gamma^(L1+2L2)=1+epsilon(2L1+4L2)+O(epsilon^2). This is a basis- and scheduling-dependent construction, not a claim that arbitrary hardware noise can be cancelled at that overhead. Algebra and a full two-qubit CNOT-plus-following-layer superoperator check are used, including reset branches.
