# Derivation

Let the Hessian of the kinetic functional be `K(q)`. Stationarity of the
quadratic energy plus the external perturbation gives

$$
K(q)\,\delta\rho(q)+\delta V(q)=0,
\qquad
\frac{\delta\rho}{\delta V}=-K(q)^{-1}.
$$

The frozen prompt simultaneously states

$$
-G(q)=C\,l(x)>0,
\qquad
\delta\rho(q)=-G(q)\delta V(q).
$$

Thus `G<0`, the frozen response coefficient `-G` is positive, and consistency
requires `K=1/G<0`. The gold instead reports `K=-1/G>0`, whose variational
response is negative. Tasks 1 and 4 therefore cannot both satisfy the frozen
contract. The 1992 source removes the contradiction by using `G=C l>0` and
`delta rho=-G delta V`.

For large `x`, direct series expansion gives

$$
l(x)=\frac{1}{3x^2}+\frac{1}{15x^4}+\frac{1}{35x^6}+O(x^{-8}),
$$

and hence

$$
l_1(x)=\frac58\left[l(x)^{-1}-3x^2+\frac35\right]
=-\frac{3}{35x^2}+O(x^{-4}).
$$

That high-momentum tail controls short-distance singular structure, not the
large-distance tail. A decisive counterexample is

$$
H(q)=\frac{1}{q^2+1}\sim q^{-2},\qquad
h(r)=\frac{e^{-r}}{4\pi r},\qquad
M_2=\int_0^\infty r^3e^{-r}\,dr=6.
$$

Therefore `q^-2` alone does not determine whether the second radial moment is
finite. Finally, bisection below the Lindhard cusp gives

$$
x_\star=0.80540452396949279727\ldots,
$$

so the printed root itself is valid even though the overall gold is not.
