## Finite oscillator bath

Write b=(a,a_1,...,a_M), H=b† h b, h00=10, hjj=omega_j, h0j=hj0=g_j. The Heisenberg equation is db/dt=-i h b, so b(t)=U(t)b(0), U=exp(-i h t). This is exact for the paper's RWA Hamiltonian at finite M, with no Born, Markov or Fock-space truncation. The isolated ancilla can freely rotate; local phase rotations do not change logarithmic negativity. A real eigendecomposition h=V diag(lambda)V^T yields U0j=sum_k V0k exp(-i lambda_k t) Vjk.

Our explicit quadrature is omega_j=j*50/M, j=1,...,M, g_j=sqrt(alpha omega_j^p exp(-omega_j/omega_c)*50/M). This is a conventional right Riemann discretization of the paper's spectral density. The source does not specify the exact frequency nodes or quadrature. We test midpoint nodes and doubling M separately, without replacing the primary source-scale result. The initial thermal state is that of the UNCOUPLED bath, exactly as stated; T=0 uses n_j=0 and otherwise n_j=1/expm1(omega_j/T). System-ancilla initial state is two-mode squeezed vacuum with r=4. hbar=kB=1.

Use covariance convention vacuum V=I. Let eta=|U00|², q=1-eta+2 sum_j |U0j|² n_j. Local phase rotations give the reduced covariance V=[[aI,cZ],[cZ,bI]], a=eta*cosh(2r)+q, b=cosh(2r), c=sqrt(eta)*sinh(2r), Z=diag(1,-1). Independent invariants: det V=(ab-c²)² and Delta_PT=a²+b²+2c². The smallest partially transposed symplectic eigenvalue is nu=(a+b-sqrt((a-b)²+4c²))/2. Avoid cancellation at r=4 using nu=2*(eta+q*cosh(2r))/(a+b+sqrt((a-b)²+4c²)). This follows from cosh²-sinh²=1, not a physical approximation. E_N=max(0,-log2(nu)). At alpha=0, q=0, eta=1 and E_N=2r/ln2.

I(E)=total variation of E minus its net decrease=2*integral max(dE/dt,0)dt. On stored samples we compute exactly 2*sum max(E[i+1]-E[i],0), including the factor two. This is a lower bound to the continuous total variation and can miss extrema. The figure labels specify rounded dt (0.0209, 0.0417); primary grid ends at last t<=21 and sensitivity tests explicitly include exact endpoint and smaller dt. ## Analytic targets and applicability

A1: For a finite-dimensional, differentiable, time-local propagator, GKSL form with nonnegative rates suffices: every short-time propagator is a limit of CPTP semigroup steps and time-ordered products remain CPTP. Conversely CP-divisibility implies conditional positivity of the infinitesimal Choi operator on the subspace orthogonal to |Phi>, giving a positive semidefinite Kossakowski matrix and hence a nonnegative-rate representation. Negative rates in a redundant, noncanonical representation alone do not establish non-Markovianity. Semigroup time homogeneity is stronger than the time-dependent CP divisibility used later in the paper. Infinite-dimensional unbounded generators need domain assumptions beyond this finite-dimensional argument.

A2/F1: If rho_SA(t2)=(E_(t2,t1) tensor id_A)rho_SA(t1), local CPTP monotonicity implies E(t2)<=E(t1). Thus any positive revival and I(E)>0 refute CP divisibility, but I(E)=0 is not a converse: the monitored trajectory may miss non-CP directions, and zero entanglement can hide dynamics. Equality/constant entanglement is allowed: the source's 'always negative/strict monotonicity' must be understood as nonpositive/nonincreasing. Initial factorization from the bath and an isolated ancilla matter. Figure 1 encodes these connections; its red/green curves are examples, not numerical predictions. The source typo rho_SE(0)=|Phi><Phi| should be rho_SA(0), as its stated construction requires. This does not change calculations.

A3: For a Hermiticity-preserving TP map the normalized Choi matrix C is Hermitian with trace 1. Therefore ||C||_1=1+2 sum_{lambda<0}|lambda|, so it equals 1 iff C>=0 iff the map is CP. The intermediate map is uniquely E(t+epsilon,0)E(t,0)^(-1) when E(t,0) is invertible. The source explicitly acknowledges singular inverses; we do not claim a universal nonsingular reconstruction. g is the right derivative at zero of this norm excess; positivity detects infinitesimal failure. Its integral and I/(1+I) are nonnegative, with correct zero/infinite limits. The pointwise sentence 'g(t)=0 iff E_(t+epsilon,t) is CP' needs the infinitesimal interpretation: a zero first derivative cannot rule out a second-order loss over finite epsilon. A time-local sign-crossing control checks this limit boundary. No optimization appears in these constructions; entanglement estimation/tomography can still have experimental cost.

A4: Dephasing has rho01(t)=exp[-2 integral_0^t gamma(s) ds] rho01(0). Over an interval the coherence multiplier is q=exp[-2 integral_t^(t+epsilon) gamma(s) ds]. Its normalized Choi eigenvalues are (1+q)/2, (1-q)/2,0,0 for real positive q. Hence norm=max(1,q), and g=max(0,-2gamma(t)), verifying Eq.(4). Divergence occurs if the negative part is nonintegrable; normalization tends to 1. gamma proportional to tan(t) has a nonintegrable negative part at its poles, and inverse-map singularities must be treated intervalwise. The brief footnote [16] spontaneous-emission analogy can be checked algebraically: for amplitude damping eta(t)>0, intermediate eta ratio R, Choi eigenvalues (1+R)/2,(1-R)/2,0,0, so g=max(0,d ln eta/dt). This agrees with revival intervals for a Lorentzian reservoir; no extra numerical figure from reference [6] is imported into the requested paper's denominator.
