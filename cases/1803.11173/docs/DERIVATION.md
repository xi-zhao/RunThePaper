## Source model and independent numerical method
The numerical paragraph specifies |psi0> = RY(pi/4)^tensor n |0>, R_P(theta)=exp(-i theta P/2), independent P uniform in X,Y,Z and theta uniform [0,2pi), followed by all nearest-neighbor CZ on an open chain. Each layer is rotations then CZ. H=Z_0 Z_1; differentiate theta of qubit 0 in layer 1. No author code or figure ordinates enter generation. Source does not state sample count, random seed or the linear-depth multiplier used for Figure 3. Figure 4 fixes even n=2,...,24 and shows depths up to 500; Figure 3 includes n=26.

The statement RY(pi/4)=sqrt(Hadamard) is false as a matrix identity (also on |0> up to phase). We select the explicit RY exponential; separately check the literal square-root alternative. The initial RY state's Bloch vector is (1/sqrt(2),0,1/sqrt(2)).

A faithful second-moment reduction avoids exponential state-vector cost without altering the ensemble. Start with H tensor H in the Heisenberg picture. A single-qubit random Pauli rotation maps a diagonal Pauli pair P tensor P (P!=I) to P tensor P with weight 2/3, and each other nonidentity pair with weight 1/6. Identity is fixed. Cross terms vanish upon the uniform angle integral. CZ permutes Pauli strings, with signs canceling between the two copies. Consequently the ensemble E[g^2] is an exactly specified classical Markov expectation on Pauli strings; this is a calculation of the same variance, not a substituted Clifford-circuit claim about higher moments. The gradient mean vanishes by integration over its periodic angle.

Backward evolution: start ZZII..., apply CZ, then ordinary local transition T for all but the final backward (first forward) rotation layer. At that last layer integrate the differentiated qubit exactly. With r_a the initial Bloch components and s_a=r_a^2, ordinary boundary b_I=1, b_a=1/6+s_a/2; derivative boundary d_I=0, d_a=(1+s_a)/6. Thus for RY initial state b=(1,5/12,1/6,5/12) and d=(0,1/4,1/6,1/4), in I,X,Y,Z order. The trajectory contribution is d_{P0} product_{j>0} b_{Pj}. For a diagnostic fixed first generator Y, d_X=d_Z=1/2 and d_I=d_Y=0. This variant is explicitly distinct from the paper's independent uniform axis rule.

CZ acts on binary symplectic labels by x'=x, z'=z XOR (x<<1) XOR (x>>1), masked to n bits. A transition T can be sampled as: with probability 1/2 leave the current nonidentity label, otherwise replace it by a uniformly chosen nonidentity label. I stays I. This gives 2/3,1/6,1/6 exactly. We check it against a deterministic 4^n transition calculation and independent continuous-angle complex state-vector gradients at small n.

The Markov chain is doubly stochastic on all nonidentity strings, connected (single-qubit Pauli changes plus nearest-neighbor CZ can grow/shrink support) and aperiodic (positive self-loop). Its stationary distribution is uniform on 4^n-1 strings. Sum d=2/3; sum b=2. Therefore the exact deep-circuit variance is [ (2/3) 2^(n-1) ]/(4^n-1) = 2^n/[3(4^n-1)]. For fixed Y it is 2^(n-1)/(4^n-1), precisely 3/2 times larger. The slope tends to -ln 2. The alternative square-root initialization also has sum s=1 and hence the same uniform-axis deep variance.

## General gradient, 1-design mean and exact Haar variance
Let D=2^n, rho of trace one, and U=A exp(-i theta V) B. Put rho_-=B rho B^dagger and H_+=exp(i theta V) A^dagger H A exp(-i theta V). Then g=i Tr rho_- [V,H_+]. Independence plus either side a 1-design makes the expectation zero, by a trace of a commutator or by H_+ proportional to I.

Equation (3) needs care with the paper's split: if W_k is kept in U_+, the generator inside the commutator must be W_k^dagger V W_k. Equivalently move W_k into U_-. The printed unrestricted W_k formula will be tested with a one-qubit example; this is a factorization convention issue, not a failure of the corrected gradient identity.

Write S_rho=Tr(rho^2)-(Tr rho)^2/D, S_H=Tr(H^2)-(Tr H)^2/D and S_V=Tr(V^2)-(Tr V)^2/D. From the two-copy twirl E[(U X U^dagger)^tensor2]=a I+b Swap, b=[Tr X^2-(Tr X)^2/D]/(D^2-1). For traceless C, E[(Tr U X U^dagger C)^2]=b Tr C^2. Therefore the exact three cases replacing Equation (7) are:

1. U_- 2-design: Var(g) = -S_rho/(D^2-1) E_{U+} Tr[V,H_+]^2.
2. U_+ 2-design: Var(g) = -S_H/(D^2-1) E_{U-} Tr[V,rho_-]^2.
3. Both independent 2-designs: Var(g)=2 D S_rho S_H S_V/(D^2-1)^2.

For a pure rho and traceless H,V, case 1 has coefficient 1/[D(D+1)] in place of the paper's 1/D^2; case 2 has Tr H^2/(D^2-1) in place of Tr H^2/D^2. Case 3 is 2 TrH^2 TrV^2/[(D-1)(D+1)^2], rather than 2 TrH^2 TrV^2/D^3. The paper's formulas are leading large-D forms for pure rho and appropriately centered observables, not exact finite-D equalities. An identity shift of H must never change a gradient; the printed formulas fail that invariant in cases 2 and 3. For H=I the exact gradient is zero.

Independent analytic check: for one qubit, V=Z/2, H=X and pure rho with U_- Haar and U_+=I, g is minus the Bloch y coordinate, so Var=1/3. Printed case 1 gives 1/2. For U_+ Haar, rho=|0><0| and V=X/2, g has variance 1/3; printed case 2 gives 1/4. With both sides Haar, variance is 2/9; printed case 3 gives 1/4. Direct Bloch-sphere integrals E r_i r_j=delta_ij/3 give these values without the twirl derivation. These are within the stated conditions, not a new model requirement.

## Scaling, assumptions and practical interpretation
For bounded local generators/observables, the exact cases yield variance O(poly(n)/2^n) under the appropriate design assumptions. Chebyshev gives Pr(|g|>=epsilon)<=Var(g)/epsilon^2. It implies exponentially small probability at fixed epsilon, not literally zero derivatives. For M polynomial terms with polynomial coefficients, Minkowski/Cauchy-Schwarz bounds sd(sum c_j g_j)<=sum |c_j| sd(g_j), so covariance cannot remove the exponential factor. This fails if operator norms or coefficients scale exponentially; their restriction is necessary. A 2-design suffices for this second-moment/Chebyshev result, not every Haar concentration statement or arbitrary correlated/adaptively chosen direction.

A single observable's variance plateau does not prove that the entire circuit ensemble is a unitary 2-design. The O(n^(1/d)) depth remark is a stated argument, not a proven upper bound for every architecture/design precision; a light-cone lower scale alone cannot establish mixing. We assess this as a limited original remark and will not manufacture a general depth theorem. Structured initialization and pretraining are proposals, not demonstrated main-text results.

With independent projective sampling of a bounded Pauli observable, additive error epsilon needs O(epsilon^-2) shots (away from deterministic outcomes). Parameter-shift gradients inherit this scale; g_rms proportional 2^(-n/2) gives shot demand of order 2^n at fixed signal-to-noise. Coherent amplitude-estimation assumptions can improve precision exponent but still give exponential n scaling. The paper's random-walk/global optimization language is an argument under noisy gradient access, not a universal oracle lower bound; analytic expectation evaluation and structured correlated searches are outside that argument.
